Sources, definitions and caveats
  • Periods: before the Fed, 1837–1913; Fed before WWII, 1914–1945 (the Fed opened in November 1914); Fed after WWII, 1946–2026. The middle period mixes the early Fed under the gold standard, the Great Depression, the arrival of deposit insurance in 1934 and WWII; the epoch table splits it at 1934.
  • Inflation: Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis, "Consumer Price Index, 1800–" (1967 = 100). Pre-1913 values are BLS reconstructions (Vermont farmers' prices to 1851, Hoover's CPI to 1890, Rees's cost of living to 1912). 2026 is the Minneapolis Fed's Q3-to-Q3 estimate.
  • Contraction dates: NBER monthly peaks and troughs, 1857–2020; Joseph Davis's (2006) annual dates for 1836–37 and 1839–40 (treated as mid-year to mid-year). NBER has dated no contraction since April 2020.
  • Depth: peak-to-trough fall in annual output, using the higher of the peak year and the year before as the peak level and the lowest year from the peak through the trough year as the trough. Output is the NBER Macrohistory manufacturing production index (1863–1918) and the annual average of the Fed's industrial production index (1919 on). Annual data on both sides keeps the comparison fair: monthly data would make post-1919 drops look deeper just by catching short dips (Christina Romer's measurement critique). Annual data understates short contractions, so several pre-1914 ones show 0%; contractions before 1863 are not measured.
  • Banking panics: Jalil (2015), plus the 1930–33 panics and the 2008 financial crisis.
  • Bank failures: 1864–1933, bank suspensions from Historical Statistics of the United States, series X-741 (includes banks that later reopened; pre-1892 figures are less complete). 1934–2026, FDIC failures of all insured institutions (FRED BKFTTLA641N), which include savings institutions in the 1980s savings-and-loan crisis; 2021–22 are entered as 0 (FRED shows no value); 2026 is year to date. Counts are not scaled by the number of banks.
